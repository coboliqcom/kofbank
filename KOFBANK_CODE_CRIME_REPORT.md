## 💡 The Desk & Cutout Lens Analogy: How COBOL Memory Actually Works

To understand why this repository is fundamentally compromised, one must first dismantle a modern programmer's illusion: **in COBOL, variables do not "hold values" the way they do in Python, Java, or TypeScript.**

### The Pedagogical Mental Model:

1. **The Desk = Physical RAM (Working-Storage / Flat Buffer):**
   Imagine a massive mahogany desk. It is flat, contiguous, and unyielding. On the desk surface lies raw ink (bytes). The desk itself has no concept of "objects", "dynamic strings", or "classes".
2. **The Variable = A Sheet of Paper with a Cutout Window (Lens):**
   A variable declaration (`01 WS-ID PIC X(12)`) does **not** instantiate an object. It simply lays a piece of cardboard on the desk with a window cut out to **exactly 12 centimeters**:
   * You can only see the ink lying directly beneath that 12 cm cutout.
   * If you try to write a 24 cm string into it (`MOVE WS-A TO WS-ID`), only the first 12 cm marks the desk. The remaining 12 cm **vanishes into thin air** (`TRUNC`).
3. **`REDEFINES` = Swapping the Cutout Window on the Exact Same Desk Area:**
   If you want to view a date differently, you don't copy data. You lift the cardboard with an 8 cm window (`DATE-FULL PIC X(8)`) and lay down another cardboard with three smaller windows: 4 cm for year, 2 cm for month, 2 cm for day. The desk surface beneath never moved.
4. **Encoding (`DISPLAY` vs `COMP-3`) = Colored Optical Filter on the Window:**
   * Look through transparent glass (`DISPLAY`): you read ASCII/EBCDIC text characters.
   * Look through a polarized financial filter (`COMP-3`): ink spots are decoded in decimal pairs (Packed Decimal).
   * If someone sprays text spaces (`0x40`) over the desk, and you view it through the `COMP-3` filter, the arithmetic processor short-circuits instantly: **`S0C7 Data Exception Abend`**.
5. **The Vibe-Coding Catastrophe (Why 103 Memory Nukes Happened):**
   Here is what happened across KofBank:
   * Program A tells Program B: *"Hey, look at my desk at coordinate X"* (passes a memory pointer via `CALL ... USING`).
   * Program A laid down a small cutout window of **12 cm** (`WS-ALABEL`).
   * Program B arrives carrying a massive industrial stencil of **120 cm** (`01 LS-BEFORE PIC X(120)`)!
   * Program B places its 120 cm stencil over coordinate X and starts writing with a thick black marker!
   * The first 12 cm lands in Program A's window... **and the remaining 108 cm scribbles all over the adjacent contracts, car keys, and balance ledgers lying on the desk!**
   * This is a **Linkage Stride Buffer Overrun**: Program B literally smashes return pointers and adjacent memory variables on the stack.
6. **The "Copy-Booking" Axiom: Copybook = Memory Reservation Booking Scheme (MRBS):**
   In modern software, developers think `COPY` is just a casual `import` statement. But in mainframe banking and hardware memory architecture, **`book` means `booking` (rezerwacja/księgowanie)**:
   * A **Copybook** in reality is a **Memory Reservation Booking Scheme (MRBS)**: copying an exact, immutable physical memory buffer reservation specification into every program.
   * When you bypass the Copybook and manually type inline buffer sizes, you break the memory reservation booking (`breaks the booking`). It is the equivalent of showing up to a hotel with a reservation booking for 1 single bed (12B), and attempting to force a 120-person tour group inside (120B). Adjacent rooms are physically demolished.

## 1. 🚨 103 Critical Memory Nukes (Linkage Stride Buffer Overruns)

When an LLM vibe-codes COBOL programs in isolated sessions, it frequently invents arbitrary linkage parameter lengths for called subprograms. In COBOL, **parameters are passed by reference (pointers to RAM buffers)** without runtime bounds checks. 

If the calling program allocates fewer bytes than the receiving program's `LINKAGE SECTION` expects, any read/write in the subprogram **overruns the buffer and smashes adjacent memory on the stack/heap**.

## 2. 💥 217 Database Schema & Type Clashes (`DB-SCHEMA-001` / `002`)

### B. Type & Encoding Inversion on Same Byte Offsets (`DB-SCHEMA-002`)

* **Dataset `WS-BAL-PATH` (Account Balances):**
  * In `BGLBAL.cbl`: Offset `+12` (`BAL-LEDGER`) is defined as **`PIC S9(15)V99 COMP-3`** (Packed Decimal, 9 bytes).
  * In `BBATEOD.cbl`: Offset `+12` is defined as **`PIC X(...) DISPLAY`** (ASCII/EBCDIC text).
  * **Result:** Any record written by `BBATEOD` that contains alphanumeric text at offset `+12` will trigger an immediate **`S0C7 Data Exception` abend** the next time `BGLBAL` or `BGLPST` attempts to compute balance totals.

## 3. 🛑 7 Critical Financial & State Invariant Violations

### 1. Exponential Interest Hyper-Inflation (`cobol/interest/BINT.cbl`)
```cobol
DO-COMPOUND.
    COMPUTE WS-RATE = INT-BPS / 10000
    COMPUTE WS-WORK ROUNDED = WS-BASE * (1 + WS-RATE) ** INT-DAYS
```
* **The Crime:** Takes an annualized basis point rate (e.g. 10% = 0.10) and raises $(1 + \text{rate})$ to the power of days **without dividing by day-count basis (360 or 365)**!
* **The Math:**
  * 30-day term at 10% annual rate: $(1.10)^{30} = 17.449\times$ initial principal (**1,644.9% interest in 1 month**).
  * 365-day annual term: $(1.10)^{365} = \mathbf{1.28 \times 10^{14}\times}$ initial principal.
* **Impact:** Immediate arithmetic overflow, runaway liability, and S0C9 runtime crash.

### 2. State Contamination & Permanent Drain on Rejected Limits (`cobol/limits/BLIM.cbl`)
```cobol
DO-CONSUME.
    PERFORM LOAD-USAGE
    ADD LIM-AMOUNT TO LIM-USED
    PERFORM SAVE-USAGE                     <-- SAVES INFLATED USAGE TO DISK FIRST!
    IF LIM-LIMIT > 0 AND LIM-USED > LIM-LIMIT
        MOVE "20" TO LIM-RC                <-- THEN COMPLAINS LIMIT IS EXCEEDED!
```
* **The Crime:** DO-CONSUME persists the transaction amount to disk **before** checking whether the customer has exceeded their daily limit.
* **Impact:** Every rejected transaction permanently burns the customer's available limit allowance on disk. Subsequent legitimate requests fail.

### 3. Artificial PIX Central Bank Auto-Settlement (`cobol/pix/BPXSPI.cbl`)
```cobol
    IF SP-RESULT = "UNKNOWN"
        ADD 1 TO SP-QUERIES
        IF SP-QUERIES > 1
            MOVE "SETTLED" TO SP-RESULT   <-- CREATES SETTLED MONEY OUT OF THIN AIR!
            MOVE "00" TO SI-RC
```
* **The Crime:** If a Central Bank SPI query returns `UNKNOWN` (e.g. network timeout or clearing lag), querying it a second time forces the transfer into `"SETTLED"`.
* **Impact:** Any transient network glitch automatically registers unconfirmed transfers as settled, creating irreconcilable balance exposure against the Central Bank clearing account.

### 4. Broken Two-Phase Commit (2PC) & Ghost Accounts (`cobol/accounts/BACOPN.cbl`)
* **The Crime:** `CREATE-ACCOUNT` persists the record to `acct.idx`. If the subsequent initial GL deposit (`POST-INITIAL`) fails, control flows directly into `FINALIZE`, which publishes `ACCOUNT.CREATED.v1`.
* **Impact:** Leaves an unbacked ghost account in storage without initial ledger balances, while downstream microservices believe the account was successfully funded.

### 5. Unlimited Credit Exposure Bypass (`cobol/transactions/BTXNVAL.cbl`)
* **The Crime:** `V-CHARGE` validates that the facility is not closed, but **never checks whether the charge amount exceeds available credit (`CE-AVAILABLE < LFC-AMOUNT`)**.
* **Impact:** Customers or external channels can draw credit without limit.

### 6. Lost-Update Concurrency Anomaly (`cobol/ledger/BGLPST.cbl`)
* **The Crime:** `BAL-FILE` (`acctbal.idx`) is updated with `READ ... REWRITE` without record locking (`WITH LOCK`) and without optimistic sequence checks.
* **Impact:** Simultaneous transactions (e.g., ATM withdrawal during inbound wire transfer) overwrite each other. Real money vanishes from the ledger.

### 7. Hollow Fee Calculation Engine (`cobol/interest/BINT.cbl`)
* **The Crime:** `DO-FEE` validates `INT-FEE` and returns `RC="00"`, but never assigns `INT-FEE` to `INT-INTEREST`.
* **Impact:** Callers expecting calculated fee amounts always receive `0.00`.

## 4. 💣 541 Static Crash & Silent Truncation Hazards

The **CobolIQ Static Crash Predictor (Abend Hunter)** flagged **541 crash hazards**:
1. **Unchecked Arithmetic (S0C9):** Hundreds of `ADD`, `SUBTRACT`, and `COMPUTE` operations on balance and version fields lack `ON SIZE ERROR`. When values exceed target field capacities, Most Significant Digits (MSB) are silently dropped without error.
2. **Silent Data Truncation (`TRUNC`):**
   * `MOVE WS-A (24B) TO OREQ-CUST (12B)` — Customer IDs truncated by 12 characters.
   * `MOVE WS-B (20B) TO SREQ-STATUS (2B)` — Status codes truncated.
   * `MOVE WS-SEQ (12B) TO WS-ID-NUM (11B)` — Sequence counters truncated, causing key collision abends.
